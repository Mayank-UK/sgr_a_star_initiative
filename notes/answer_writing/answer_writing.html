- What UPSC wants in answers (from the evaluators and toppers) - Basic information about UPSC evaluators - UPSC selects examiners with long academic or administrative careers, so younger people aren’t usually included. - Examiners are not UPSC employees; they are invited from a national panel. - Top 10 copies are re-evaluated to check and confirm the marks granted by every examiner; they also do some random checks on other copies checked by the examiner, which is done by a third examiner - Language & presentation - Language should be very direct, crisp and clear to display very good conceptual clarity - It should neither be too simple from the perspective of formal writing, nor too poetic or difficult to understand - Explaining the concepts in easy language represents clarity of thought. Use a single sentence to convey a single idea; don't try to fuse multiple ideas in a confusing way - Strictly avoid ambiguous and verbosity - Ending each point with "...this derailed/stopped/halted the planning process", and not using innovative and diverse language, this shows lack of content - Good handwriting - Handwriting should be very good and easy to understand. Information should be arranged in an easy-to-consume form. The examiner is very impressed. - Bad handwriting leads to difficulty in reading and conforming numerical data, for example 2026 and 2016 both looking same, and examiner is confused - Grammar and vocabulary - Keep grammar sorted, keep vocabulary balanced, never use slang or unprofessional language, keep it professional, try to be direct, avoid using advanced poetic English - Bad english changes the meaning of sentences, like using "NPA's instead of NPAs", "Period review instead of Periodic review", "Police instead of policy", etc, leading examiner in confusion and even mis-interpretation of the context - Do not use unprofessional english like using "b/w instead of between", using symbols instead of words, drawing up/down arrows to show increment/decrement, words like "honeymoon period", etc - Do not use forceful words like "should", "must", etc, and instead use milder and suggestive words like "may", "might", "can", "could", "possible", etc - Using points instead of paragraphs - Avoid using paragraphs, answer in points - Try connecting points the points with each other, keep the whole answer in harmony from intro to conclusion - Use of abbreviations - Examiner admires when full form is provided of the term in first occurrence, and later using short form, this shows you are not trying to write to fill space - Use of design principle - Information should in hierarchy - Information should be arranged in proper hierarchy, use indentation and highlighting for establishing hierarchy - Good alignment - Text and diagrams should alignment horizontally and vertically - Highlighting - Highlight by underlining and boxing the keywords and data points to catch the attention of the examiner - Spacing - Congested information without proper spacing is very difficult to read and looks unorganized - Diagrams - Use flowcharts, graphs, charts, and other diagrams to represent numerical and geographical data visually to improve presentation - Extract out from topper notes and copies - Use boxes to contain the diagrams, mind maps and flow chart - Avoid distributed information - Mandatorily draw diagrams in every geography answer - Showing evolution using timeline diagram - Not every question requires a diagram like some questions of history, polity, etc, avoid forceful integration of diagrams in every question - No need to be extra creative, just meet the basic demands of the question - Intent - Do not at any cost write anything sensitive to that looks to be emotionally manipulating them by hints of religion, caste, or any other ideology. even the candidature could be cancelled - Examiner should not find any manipulation, bias based on religion, caste, gender, social status, etc. This can even disqualify the candidature - Any hint of language disrespecting any figure, be it good or bad from personal perspective can lead to deduction in marks, specially in case in case of important figures like Mahatma Gandhi, Bhagat Singh, etc - Content - There won't be any time for analyzing and thinking; a speed of 30 wpm has to be maintained, and analysis has to be done beforehand - Keep the content ready and structured before hand to replicate in the exam - Meeting the demand of the question - Be very specific to address the demand of the question - Here a lot students fall into the trap of putting information they remember and not what is asked - Avoid forcefully pushing generic current affairs information like "Trump trade war with India" - Forgetting to include the fundamental information subconsciously due to habit of only giving sensational current affairs information, like forgetting to include the date of 15 Aug in a relevant question just because it's too known - Complete the paper - Completing the paper is half the battle won, without even leaving half a page empty, this doesn't mean to fill it with irrelevant information, filling with irrelevant information will impact examiners viewpoint on other answers - Divide the whole time equally between each question, don't get emotionally attached to any question, attempt within time and move on (Anudeep Durishetty) - Margin between very good answers and average answers is very less, but time difference is huge, the goal is to attempt and answer all questions (Anudeep Durishetty) - Don't leave any question, write at least something generic around the location, related concepts, etc - UPSC is checking if you can come up with a solution when faced with adversity - Don't leave empty space in between answer in hope to fill it later, if left blank, it leads to subconscious impression of incomplete answer and lack of knowledge - Try to cover the whole space, use strategies like diagrams, flows, etc - Not all questions are to be perfectly answered, write an average answer and move on, don't waste more than allotted time on each question - No bluffing - No bluffing by incorrect facts, whole paper will go down the drain - This will lead to internal negative marking by the examiner while assigning marks - Preference for formal data over informal data - For formal definitions, if proper cited definition is available by a person then use that and cite him, like definition of the constitution - Few subjects are very critical for the play of words like Polity, in polity the words, definitions are given much more relevant if they are cited from Supreme court, any other authority, or simply "according to oxford/cambridge", etc - Strictly avoid giving personal & emotional thoughts, perspectives, and analysis in GS papers, when not asked - Each and every generic statement should be backed by data - They dislike purely theoretical content and repetition of coaching language - It's best to quote the source of data along with fact like census data, etc - Back each and every argument with data, by official data of the government, an organization, or any other body, or a sensational current affair, the data should support the argument - They love India-specific, current affairs-integrated answers - Increasing specificity of generic data - Give good amount of relevant facts, names of people, policies, bodies, current affairs, etc to increase specificity - Add year with each current affairs, schemes, other data point, and box it - In case of making an ambiguous point more specific, integrate it with some data point like below: - "Improving waste management to solve the issue" -> "Taking inspiration from Sweden waste management model for efficient waste management" - In the above line, it was just an assumption based on the fact that Sweden is doing good in this domain, no one can't cancel this point, and it will make this point look very specific - Use of "keywords" and "punch lines" - Toppers are again and again reminding us to collect and use keywords in the answers to make striking points - Use of trending "keywords" from supreme court, international relation, science and tech, etc - Use of punch lines to make an impact and make an answer interesting to read, like "Rusted steel frame to a trusted steel frame" - Collect from PYQs and toppers - Structure based on priority of information - Put most important points in the beginning, and weaker points towards middle - Inclusion of critical information - Examiner is actively searching for these keywords to estimate the minimum threshold knowledge of the candidate - Missing out on the major elements from the topic leads to loss of marks, for example missing "bronze sculptures" in chola topic, missing very close and famous current affairs regarding a topic - Giving extra - Give a little extra than others in terms of examples, diagrams, etc, to fetch 1/2 mark extra over for each answer, leading to margin of 10 marks for the whole paper - 1 mark less can shift rank upto 10-20 places - Avoid using repetitive information points and using same data in a lot of questions - Recall failure strategies - Air 75 Ketan Shukla advice to not waste time in remembering exact complex names for misc topics, just use custom alternate title like "Sweden model", "England model", etc - "Farmer protest (2020)" can be used instead the exact name of the "Andolan" - Strictly abstaining from intermixing of the information due to improper recall - Ethics and Essays - They want to test your reasoning, aptitude, and combined personality via ethics and essay, so make sure to keep it little personal to help them out - Holistic approach - A holistic and wholesome answer with multiple dimensions over depth on few dimensions - UPSC demands interdisciplinary thinking, the answer should cover polity, science, ethics, economics, etc - Include broad spectrum of examples and not limiting at a particular caste, religion, sect, gender, etc - Showing neutrality in answer and not taking sides - Give both aspects of the answer if it asks to agree, not simply agree, stay neutral, give both sides, and align 60% with question - Avoid too much criticism and appreciation of government, policies, or any other topic - They expect: - No ideological bias - No extreme views - No populism - No political opinions - No emotional arguments - Unique but known examples (value addition) - Use examples which can be understood by the examiner immediately, but still rarely used by other candidates, this shows that you have not mugged up coaching material - Like using example of statue of liberty, what it signifies with its posture, sword, and flame, this example will be very unique and still be very recognizable to the examiner - Using names of people, facts, and other data which are part of PYQs and very famous news is going to be sweet spot - Try to balance cutting-edge current affairs and traditional famous current affairs. Examiners will be hesitant to give marks if the information is too narrow to be understood by them - While this is true for GS, it is not for optional, as optional is checked by subject experts, they appreciate minute details - Showcase pragmatic approch whenever possible - Avoid writing impractical and generic suggestions in the way forward - They reward “On-the-ground” governance perspective with ethical considerations - Using framework to generate answers rapidly -
