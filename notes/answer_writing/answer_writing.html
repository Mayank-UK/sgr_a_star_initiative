Introduction 2. Body 3. Conclusion - Language & presentation - Language should be very direct, crisp and clear to display very good conceptual clarity - It should neither be too simple from the perspective of formal writing, nor too poetic or difficult to understand - Explaining the concepts in easy language represents clarity of thought. Use a single sentence to convey a single idea; don't try to fuse multiple ideas in a confusing way - Strictly avoid ambiguous and verbosity - Ending each point with "...this derailed/stopped/halted the planning process", and not using innovative and diverse language, this shows lack of content - Good handwriting - Handwriting should be very good and easy to understand. Information should be arranged in an easy-to-consume form. The examiner is very impressed. - Bad handwriting leads to difficulty in reading and conforming numerical data, for example 2026 and 2016 both looking same, and examiner is confused - Grammar and vocabulary - Keep grammar sorted, keep vocabulary balanced, never use slang or unprofessional language, keep it professional, try to be direct, avoid using advanced poetic English - Bad english changes the meaning of sentences, like using "NPA's instead of NPAs", "Period review instead of Periodic review", "Police instead of policy", etc, leading examiner in confusion and even mis-interpretation of the context - Do not use unprofessional english like using "b/w instead of between", using symbols instead of words, drawing up/down arrows to show increment/decrement, words like "honeymoon period", etc - Do not use forceful words like "should", "must", etc, and instead use milder and suggestive words like "may", "might", "can", "could", "possible", etc - Do not use disrespectful words like killed for freedom fighters, use "martyr" - Using points instead of paragraphs - Avoid using paragraphs, answer in points - Try connecting points the points with each other, keep the whole answer in harmony from intro to conclusion - Example of continuos points using free trade topic - 1. Leading to the fall of businesses - 2. The fall of businesses leads to unemployment - 3. Unemployment leads to social unrest - 4. Social unrest leading to political instability - Use of abbreviations - Examiner admires when full form is provided of the term in first occurrence, and later using short form, this shows you are not trying to write to fill space -
