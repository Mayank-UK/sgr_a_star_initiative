Generic analysis - There are only few types of pattern on which UPSC frames it's questions, these patterns can be mastered for accuracy and time - If the statements use words with possibility like "may", "can" etc, then that statement is usually right, and if there is use of extreme words like "cannot", "impossible", "always" etc, then that statement is usually wrong - Example: - Statements: - 1. The Parliament of India can place a particular law in the Ninth Schedule of the Constitution of India - 2. The validity of a law placed in the Ninth Schedule cannot be examined by any court and no judgement can be made on it - Options: - a. 1 only - b. 2 only - c. Both 1 and 2 - d. Niether 1 and 2 - Analysis: - Statement 1 shows possiblity and statement 2 shows extreme nature, making statement 1 only highly probable statement - So, b is the correct answer - If the cover of a question is very broad, then it usually it covers most of the statements except extreme ones - Example: - In desert which type of leave adaptation? - Statements: - 1. Hard and waxy - 2. Small leave size - 3. Thorns instead of leaves - Options: - a. 2 and 3 only - b. 2 only - c. 3 only - d. 1, 2 and 3 - Analysis: - This is a nature question, which can be analyzed accurately using first principles thinking - To avoid water loss -> reduce surface area -> small size -> leading to thorns in some cases - Also, some known data about cactus like plants in desert which helps with all above statements - Understanding the words deeply to analyze the statements - Example: - How is the National Green Tribunal (NGT) different from the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB)? - Statements: - 1. The NGT has been established by an Act whereas the CPCB has been created by an executive order of the Government - 2. The NGT provides environmental justice and helps reduce the burden of litigation in the higher courts whereas the CPCR promotes cleanliness of streams and wells, and aims to improve the quality of air in the country - Options: - a. 1 only - b. 2 only - b. Both 1 and 2 - b. Neither 1 nor 2 - Analysis: - By understanding the meaning of words, we know that "Tribunal" is a body established to settle certain types of disputes and Board is usually for recommendation in polity sense - This above analysis makes option 2 highly probable of being right, now the confusion is only statement 1 which is dependent on some knowledge - In the above case, b is correct - (Fallible with trend) If two statements are very close relating to a particular topic, then one of them is usually wrong, giving the possibility of eliminating the option which contains both these statements - Example 1: - Which are right? - 1.
