- Question attempt checklist to avoid making mistakes - 1. Did I read the question word by word? - Watch for (and underline): - NOT / EXCEPT / ONLY / ALL / CORRECT / INCORRECT. - Numbers and qualifiers like “all of the above,” “none of the above,” “always,” “never.” - Underline/mentally highlight such trigger words. - 2. Do I actually know the core concept? - If yes → proceed. - If half-sure → mark for review, don’t guess impulsively. - If no → apply elimination (see below). - 3. Write each alphabet individually and apply right or wrong - Eliminate the most obviously wrong options first. - Compare remaining two with logic. UPSC often sets two close options; subtle word difference matters. - If data/numbers are in options → UPSC usually avoids extreme figures (e.g., “100%”, “never”). - 4. Confirm - Before marking, ask: - Am I answering what’s actually asked (correct vs incorrect)? - Final check: - Mentally read Question → Option → Answer once fully before darkening OMR. - MCQ Solving - MCQ solving for UPSC requires a fine balance between knowledge and aptitude, without aptitude knowledge alone won't suffice - With knowledge and practice, you will have to develop a natural aptitude for MCQ solving - MCQ solving is like playing cricket, most of the time you have to be on back-foot and play carefully, but then sometime you have to some to the front-foot with unshakable confidence to hit that six - Smart elimination technique is critical to meet the cutoff; 65% questions have to be guessed - Need to attempt 95+ questions based on intuition to meet the cutoff - Understand question framing by UPSC, very critical - Write it down structurally with examples - Do fundamental reasoning using first principle approach based on options - Read the question carefully 2 times, I often read questions wrong - Need to prepare as much information as possible for having context - For some CSAT MCQs, taking examples can be helpful to reach a solution in maths - If the statements use words with possibility like "may", "can" etc, then that statement is usually right, and if there is use of extreme words like "cannot", "impossible", "always" etc, then that statement is usually wrong - Example: - Statements: - 1. The Parliament of India can place a particular law in the Ninth Schedule of the Constitution of India - 2. The validity of a law placed in the Ninth Schedule cannot be examined by any court and no judgement can be made on it - Options: - a. 1 only - b. 2 only - c. Both 1 and 2 - d. Niether 1 and 2 - Analysis: - Statement 1 shows possiblity and statement 2 shows extreme nature, making statement 1 only highly probable statement - So, b is the correct answer - If the cover of a question is very broad, then it usually it covers most of the statements except extreme ones - Example: - In desert which type of leave adaptation? - Statements: - 1. Hard and waxy - 2. Small leave size - 3. Thorns instead of leaves - Options: - a. 2 and 3 only - b. 2 only - c. 3 only - d. 1, 2 and 3 - Analysis: - This is a nature question, which can be analyzed accurately using first principles thinking - To avoid water loss -> reduce surface area -> small size -> leading to thorns in some cases - Also, some known data about cactus like plants in desert which helps with all above statements - Understanding the words deeply to analyze the statements - Example: - How is the National Green Tribunal (NGT) different from the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB)? - Statements: - 1. The NGT has been established by an Act whereas the CPCB has been created by an executive order of the Government - 2. The NGT provides environmental justice and helps reduce the burden of litigation in the higher courts whereas the CPCR promotes cleanliness of streams and wells, and aims to improve the quality of air in the country - Options: - a. 1 only - b. 2 only - b. Both 1 and 2 - b. Neither 1 nor 2 - Analysis: - By understanding the meaning of words, we know that "Tribunal" is a body established to settle certain types of disputes and Board is usually for recommendation in polity sense - This above analysis makes option 2 highly probable of being right, now the confusion is only statement 1 which is dependent on some knowledge - In the above case, b is correct - (Fallible with trend) If two statements are very close relating to a particular topic, then one of them is usually wrong, giving the possibility of eliminating the option which contains both these statements - Example 1: - Which are right? - 1. In first Lok Sabha, the single largest party in opposition was swatantra party - 2. In the Lok Sabha, a "Leader of the Opposition" was recognized for the first time in 1960 - 3. In the Lok Sabha, if a party does not have a minimum of 76 members, it's leader cannot be recognized as the leader of the opposition - Options: - a. 1 and 3 only - b. 2 only - c. 2 and 3 only - d. 1, 2 and 3 - Analysis: - Two statements are related to leader of opposition, from the past analysis trend, most probably 1 should be correct - On the basis of some remebered data, there is a high probability statement 3 is wrong, which eliminates options a, c and d - Example 2: - Some survey data below: - Statements: - 1. Rajasthan has the highest percentage share of agricultural households among its rural households - 2. Out of the total agricultural households in the country, a little over 60 percent belong to OBCs - 3. In Kerala, a little over 60 percent of agricultural households reported to have received maximum income from sources other than agricultural activities - Options: - a. 2 and 3 only - b. 2 only - c. 1 and 3 only - d. 1, 2 and 3 - Analysis - Two statements are very close 2 and 3, both stating some 60 percent fact, high probability one of them is wrong from UPSC trends - From the known data about Kerala, we know that probabilty of 3. being right is high, as Kerala has one of the highest literacy rate in India, recieves one of the highest remittance from abroad, which leads to 2 being incorrect - But still, there is possibility of 2 being correct as it involves data about OBC which is backward and may be dependent heavily on agriculture, but we will go with previous observed trend of UPSC and eliminate optino a, b and d due to presence of statement 2 - In a multiple option type question, if a statement is looking right, then best way to confirm this is that if it is present in most of the options, then it's most probably right, and the option which do not contain it is wrong, example question 123 from Ancient India, in this example, statement 2 and 3 are present in 3 options but statemtent 1 is present only in 1 - Example: - With reference to the religious history of India, consider the following statements: - Statements: 1.
