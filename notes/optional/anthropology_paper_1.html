- To be sorted - Human sacrifice tribe How Meriah (Khond Human Sacrifice) Was Performed The Khonds believed human sacrifice was essential to appease the Earth Goddess (Tari Pen) for fertility, rain, and community protection. 1. Who Was Sacrificed? Called Meriah victims. Sources: Prisoners of war Bought children from slave traders Children gifted by other tribes Rarely, volunteers Victims were treated with great honour, well-fed, and respected before sacrifice — they were considered sacred. 2. Ritual Procedure (Step‑by‑Step) A. Long Preparatory Ritual Rituals could last several days. The victim was bathed, decorated with new clothes, turmeric, flowers. Songs and dances were performed around the Meriah. B. Procession to the Sacred Grove The community took the Meriah to a sacred grove (the Darni Penu grove). Drums, chanting, dancing accompanied the procession. The victim was given liquor or opium-like herbs to calm them. C. The Actual Sacrifice Multiple variations existed, but the classic method recorded consistently by British officers was: Method 1 (Most Recorded): Strangulation A ritual rope made of sāl bark fibres was wound around the victim's neck. Several men pulled at once so no single person was solely responsible. This was seen as transferring the life-force (puruṣa) to the Earth Goddess. Method 2: Breaking the Bones (Less Common, Earlier Tradition) Victim sometimes laid on a wooden frame. Limbs were gradually broken in a ceremonial manner. The idea was to release life force slowly into the soil. Method 3: Cutting the Throat (Rare, Later Alternative) With influence from neighbouring tribes, some groups slit the throat. Important Point: Blood was considered sacred manure. The belief was: "Human life-force ensures fertility of fields and prosperity." 3. Post‑Sacrifice Ritual (Very Important for UPSC) A. Distribution of Flesh The body was cut into pieces after death. Small portions were distributed to all clans. Each clan buried a tiny piece in their agricultural fields. This was the core of the belief system: → The land absorbs the Meriah’s life-force = Fertile harvest. B. Bones and ashes Bones sometimes buried near sacred groves. Ash mixed with seeds for sowing.
