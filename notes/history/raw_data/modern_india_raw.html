Advent of European in India
● In the 15th century, the Ottoman Empire seized control of the sea route to India through the Mediterranean , making it difficult for Europeans to reach India by their traditional route.
● This prompted European countries to compete with one other from the 15th century onwards to discover new maritime routes to India. This quest for new routes was fuelled by a strong desire to do trade with India, which was regarded as the land abundant with superior goods.
● By the 17th century, all European nations had established commercial empires in India. Their operations were primarily focused on coastal port cities in India and did not infringe upon the sovereignty of indigenous polities.
● They utilised existing trade networks to exchange goods for bullion, shifing the balance of foreign trade in Indiaʼs favour and feeling economic growth.
● However, conflicts emerged among these European powers as they sought to secure trade monopolies and territorial control. By the 1750ʼs British East India Company emerged as a dominant force in India, surpassing all other European trading companies.

Rise of British Supremacy Carnatic Wars: The Anglo-French Rivalry
Rise of British Supremacy Wars of Carnatic
● The mid-18th century saw the gradual transformation of the English East India Company
from a trading company into a political power. During the period 1600 to 1744, the
slowly expanded its trade network in India, simultaneously easing out the rivals though a
strategy combining diplomacy and war.
● Though the rise of British supremacy in India is usually traced to 1757 with the British
victory in the Battle of Plassey, the ground for the victory was laid in South India where
the British might was successfully tested out against the French Company.
 
 
Anglo-French Struggle for Supremacy - The Carnatic Wars
Why did these foreign powers start gaining Political Power ?
● When the decline of Mughal Authority (Nadir Shah Invasion of 1740) and the weak
regional powers (Marathas - Nizam of Hyderabad) proved incapable of safeguarding
the interests of European companies in India, they saw the necessity to empower
themselves militarily. 
● Further, they were commercial entities, seeking to maximize their profit margins, which
necessitated the elimination of competition and establishment of monopoly.
● Not that they only wanted to monopolize their trade and sell dear, but they also wanted to
acquire power, necessitating the acquisition of some political control over the
country they traded with.
Why this rivalry emerged in the South India
● In this context, the emergence of hostilities between the English and the French was but
natural. South India emerged as the arena for Anglo-French rivalry as it had become the
main centre for French activities with Pondicherry as capital.
● The French had also begun to wield influence in the neighbouring states of Hyderabad
and Mysore. Besides, there was not much scope in western India because of powerful
Marathas, while Eastern India was under the direct control of Alivardi Khan, therefore it
was only in Southern India that conditions became favourable, especially after the death
of Nizam-ul-Mulk of Hyderabad in 1748.

● In this war, Britain and France joined opposite camps. Perhaps conscious of their
relatively weaker position, the French tried hard to prevent the extension of war to
India and Dupleix (The French General) advised his English counterpart to maintain
neutrality.

○ In return, the Emperor issued a Farman dated 12 August 1765 granted to the Company

○ Going by this strategy, Company gained Diwani from the Emperor and Niamat

○ In this way, the infamous dual system was ʻdualʼ only in theory, while in practice the
Company acquired all real power in Bengal. The Nawab lost all independent

Dual System of Administration in Bengal Dyarchy or Double Governance system
● Dual System meant the system of Governance where the Company exercised Diwani functions
while the Nawab of Bengal exercised the Nizamat functions (defence, Law and order and
administration of justice).
● However, Nizam-ud-Daulah was allowed to succeed as Nawab of Bengal only on the condition
that he surrendered the Nizamat functions. Hence in reality, Dual System was just a sham as it
was company that exercised all political power and merely used the Nawab and his
administrate as an instrument for its purposes.
● Thus, in practice, dual system referred to the system of Government whereby the Company
acquired all real power while the ʻresponsibilityʼ for administration rested on the shoulders
 
 
of the Nawab. A fatal divorce of power form responsibility was inherent in this dual system.
● The advantage of this system was that British had power without responsibility. This had
adverse consequences for the people of Bengal, as neither the company nor the Nawab
cared for their welfare.
● In the words of Clive himself - ʻSuch a state of confusion, bribery, corruption and extortion
was never seen or head of in any country but Bengalʼ
● Thus began the Drain of Wealth from Bengal.
○ The Company stopped sending money from England to buy Indian goods. These
goods were now purchased form the revenue of Bengal and sold abroad.
● This Drain of Wealth greatly improvised Bengal and was a major factor responsible for the
terrible Bengal famine of 1770. Though the famine was caused by failure of rains, the
devastating effects were accentuated by policies of British.
● Due to the Dual System of Government, The English Company acquired a real power without
any responsibility while the position of Nawab was virtually reduced to a cipher.
● Other Adverse impacts include -
○ Disruption of trade and Commerce - Company merchants exercised strict monopoly
and would undersell the Indian merchants in Bengal, pushing many of them in
destitution and beggary.
○ It ruined Indigenous Industry and skill - Company used its political power to compel
the cotton weavers of Bengal to buy raw material from and sell woven cloths to the
Company exclusively at arbitrary prices. The Company also encouraged the industries
selectively.
Significance of Battle of Buxar
● Afer this battle, the English emerged as the supreme power. Now the Nawab of Bengal was
their puppet, the Nawab of Awadh a pliant ally and the Mughal Emperor their pensioner.
● Lands from Bengal upto Allahabad lay at their feet and the roads to Delhi were open.
● While the Plassey made them, a powerful factor in Bengal politics, Buxar made them a
powerful factor in the politics of north India.
● Battle of Plassey decided the fate of Bengal while Battle of Buxar decided the Fate of India.
○ In fact, the British now emerged as strong contenders for the Indian Empire.

Annexation of Sindh
Context of Anglo-French Rivalry
● After 1807, The Anglo French rivalry shifted to North West. After the French defeat in
naval conflict, Napoleon planned to invade India via land route.
● Alarmed by this, various diplomatic missions were sent to the frontier states and
likewise Selton mission was sent to Sindh by Lord Minto, with the help of these
diplomatic missions, friendly treaties were concluded with the respective governments
to counter French Challenge.
● Thus, in 1809, The Treaty of Eternal Friendship was signed with the Amirs, who
promised not to allow the French to settle in Sindh. In 1820, the treaty was renewed
further to exclude Americans also from Sindh.
● But soon the commercial and navigational value of the Indus began to attract the
attention of the Company’s authorities, therefore a new treaty was signed in 1832 between
the Sindh and the English (Lord Bentick) according to which -
○ Amirs allowed a free passage to English travellers and merchants thorough
Sindh and also allowed the use of Indus for commercial pursuits.
○ No English merchant would settle down in Sindh, and passports would be
needed for travellers.
● During the Great Game, Sindh was seen from a larger perspective of defence for Indian
from the Russian threat. Therefore, Sindh was a necessary prelude to annexation
of Afghanistan. Thus Sindh was conquered to counter Russia.
● Soon Ranjit Singh captured Rojhan, a town on the Sindh frontier giving the Company a
chance to offer protection to the Amirs. The Amirs had neither desired nor asked for

British Resident at Hyderabad. Therefore the Amirs had virtually passed under British

not want.
