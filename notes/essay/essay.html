- UPSC Essay Writing – Guidelines & Rules - Understanding the UPSC Essay Paper - Carries 250 marks in Mains (125 marks per essay). - Two essays to be written in 3 hours (90 minutes per essay). - Usually 4 topics in Section A and 4 in Section B — choose 1 from each. - Topics can be: - Philosophical (e.g., Happiness is not an ideal of reason, but of imagination). - Socio-political (e.g., Women empowerment and inclusive growth). - Economic (e.g., India’s path to becoming a $5 trillion economy). - Science & technology related (e.g., AI: Boon or bane for humanity). - Environment & ethics-based (e.g., Sustainable development vs. economic growth). - Tests: - Depth of understanding through factual and analytical content. - Clarity of thought with logical progression. - Ability to present multiple perspectives (social, economic, ethical). - Balanced reasoning with optimism and vision. - Rules & Expectations - Language: - Simple, clear, and grammatically correct; avoid complex vocabulary. - Avoid jargon unless essential; define technical terms (e.g., GDP, SDGs) briefly. - Word limit: - Recommended: 1000–1200 words per essay (8–10 pages). - Keep paragraphs short (5–7 lines, 100–150 words). - Structure: - No bullet points; use continuous prose in paragraphs. - Avoid excessive quotations; use 1–2 relevant quotes, explained in context. - UPSC evaluators look for essays that are well-structured (introduction-body-conclusion), balanced (covering historical, economic, social, environmental, and ethical angles), substantiated with examples, quotes, and data, and written in clear, concise, and eloquent language. - Approach: - Maintain balance; present both sides (e.g., pros and cons) before concluding. - Avoid bias, extreme opinions, or politically sensitive statements. - Support arguments with facts, examples, and case studies. - Integrate multiple dimensions - historical, social, political, economic, ethical, cultural. - Structure of the Essay - Introduction - Should be engaging, relevant, and set the tone for the essay. - Possible opening styles: - Statistics (e.g., India’s 7.5% GDP growth for economic topics). - Thought-provoking question (e.g., Can technology replace human empathy?). - Clearly state your interpretation of the topic and outline the essay’s scope. - Body - Organize ideas in a logical sequence using 3–4 sub-themes. - Present multiple perspectives: - Historical background (e.g., Indus Valley trade for economic topics). - Current scenario (e.g., India’s renewable energy push for environment). - Challenges/Problems (e.g., Digital divide in technology adoption). - Impacts (positive/negative) (e.g., Job creation vs. automation in AI). - Comparative/global examples (e.g., Japan’s bullet trains for infrastructure). - Provide supporting evidence: - Data from reports (e.g., NITI Aayog’s poverty reduction stats, UN SDGs). - Laws/policies (e.g., Right to Education Act, Swachh Bharat Mission). - Examples from history (e.g., Ashoka’s dhamma for ethical governance). - Current events (e.g., India’s G20 presidency in 2023). - Use connectors (e.g., however, consequently, in contrast) for smooth transitions. - Conclusion - Summarize main arguments concisely (1–2 paragraphs). - Provide a visionary, optimistic outlook (e.g., India as a global leader by 2047). - Avoid introducing new arguments or facts. - Possible endings: - A relevant quote (e.g., Tagore’s “Where the mind is without fear”). - A future roadmap (e.g., Achieving carbon neutrality by 2070). - A moral message (e.g., Unity in diversity as India’s strength). - Planning Before Writing - Step 1: - Read all topics carefully; identify the one with broadest scope. -
