Time Location Event Name Basic Details
1947 Pan-India Independence of India
  • Overview: India gains independence on August 15, 1947, via Indian Independence Act.
  • Significance: Ends British rule; Jawaharlal Nehru becomes first PM, delivers “Tryst with Destiny” speech.
  • Key Figures: Nehru, Lord Mountbatten, Mahatma Gandhi.
  • Note: Marks dominion status, linked to partition .
1947–1948 North-West and East India Partition of India
  • Overview: India divided into India and Pakistan per Mountbatten Plan (June 1947).
  • Significance: Displaced 15M, ~1M deaths; communal riots in Punjab, Bengal; Kashmir conflict begins.
  • Key Figures: M.A. Jinnah, Vallabhbhai Patel, Gandhi.
  • Note: Two-Nation Theory outcome, key in polity .
1947–1949 Pan-India Integration of Princely States
  • Overview: 565 princely states integrated into Indian Union.
  • Significance: Unified India; Hyderabad annexed (Operation Polo, 1948); Kashmir accedes.
  • Key Figures: Patel, V.P. Menon.
  • Note: Patel’s diplomacy key, contrast with Kashmir issue.
1948 Delhi Gandhi’s Assassination
  • Overview: Mahatma Gandhi assassinated on January 30 by Nathuram Godse.
  • Significance: National mourning; exposed communal tensions; RSS briefly banned.
  • Key Figures: Gandhi, Godse, Nehru.
  • Note: Post-partition communal challenges (April 19, 2025).
1946–1949 Delhi Drafting of Constitution
  • Overview: Constituent Assembly drafts Constitution, starting December 1946.
  • Significance: World’s longest written Constitution; adopted November 26, 1949.
  • Key Figures: B.R. Ambedkar, Rajendra Prasad.
  • Note: Ambedkar’s role central in polity questions.
1950 Pan-India Implementation of Constitution
  • Overview: Constitution enacted on January 26, 1950, making India a Republic.
  • Significance: Ended dominion status; introduced universal suffrage, federalism.
  • Key Figures: Rajendra Prasad (first President), Nehru.
  • Note: Republic Day origin, fundamental rights in syllabus.
1951–1952 Pan-India First General Elections
  • Overview: India’s first elections held under universal adult suffrage.
  • Significance: Congress wins 364/489 Lok Sabha seats; world’s largest democratic exercise.
  • Key Figures: Nehru, Sukumar Sen (Election Commission).
  • Note: Strengthened democracy, key in polity.
1955 Pan-India Hindu Marriage Act
  • Overview: Hindu Marriage Act passed, reforming marriage laws.
  • Significance: Legalized divorce, raised marriage age, promoted gender equality.
  • Key Figures: Nehru, Ambedkar.
  • Note: Social reform, part of Hindu Code Bills.
1956 Pan-India Linguistic Reorganization of States
  • Overview: States Reorganisation Act creates states based on language.
  • Significance: Formed 14 states, 6 UTs; resolved regional demands.
  • Key Figures: Nehru, Fazl Ali (SRC Chairman).
  • Note: Federalism and linguistic diversity in governance.
1961 Goa Liberation of Goa
  • Overview: Operation Vijay frees Goa from Portuguese rule.
  • Significance: Ended 450 years of colonialism; integrated Goa, Daman, Diu.
  • Key Figures: Nehru, Indian Armed Forces.
  • Note: Decolonization, linked to foreign policy.
1962 Arunachal Pradesh, Ladakh Indo-China War
  • Overview: China defeats India in border war over Aksai Chin, NEFA.
  • Significance: Exposed military weaknesses; led to defense modernization.
  • Key Figures: Nehru, Zhou Enlai.
  • Note: Impacts foreign policy, non-alignment debates.
1965 Jammu and Kashmir Indo-Pak War
  • Overview: Second Indo-Pak war over Kashmir; ends in Tashkent Agreement.
  • Significance: Stalemate; strengthened Indian military resolve.
  • Key Figures: Lal Bahadur Shastri, Ayub Khan.
  • Note: Tashkent Agreement key in diplomacy questions.
1966–1977 Punjab, Haryana Green Revolution
  • Overview: High-yield variety (HYV) seeds transform agriculture.
  • Significance: Food self-sufficiency; wheat production rose from 11M to 20M tonnes.
  • Key Figures: M.S. Swaminathan, Indira Gandhi, Norman Borlaug.
  • Note: Agricultural reform, economic transformation.
1969 Delhi Bank Nationalization
  • Overview: Indira Gandhi nationalizes 14 major banks.
  • Significance: Expanded rural banking; supported agricultural credit.
  • Key Figures: Indira Gandhi, Morarji Desai.
  • Note: Economic policy, socialist agenda in syllabus.
1971 Delhi Congress Party Split
  • Overview: Congress splits into Congress (R) and Congress (O).
  • Significance: Indira’s Congress (R) wins 1971 elections, consolidating power.
  • Key Figures: Indira Gandhi, Morarji Desai.
  • Note: Political realignment, impacts polity.
1971 East Pakistan (Bangladesh) Bangladesh Liberation War
  • Overview: India aids Mukti Bahini; defeats Pakistan, creating Bangladesh.
  • Significance: 93,000 Pakistani troops surrender; geopolitical shift.
  • Key Figures: Indira Gandhi, Sam Manekshaw, Sheikh Mujibur Rahman.
  • Note: Foreign policy, Indo-Pak relations.
1971–1980s Gujarat White Revolution
  • Overview: Operation Flood boosts dairy production via cooperatives.
  • Significance: India becomes world’s largest milk producer.
  • Key Figures: Verghese Kurien, Amul, NDDB.
  • Note: Cooperative movement, rural economy.
1974 Pokhran, Rajasthan Smiling Buddha Nuclear Test
  • Overview: India conducts first nuclear test.
  • Significance: Established nuclear capability; faced global sanctions.
  • Key Figures: Indira Gandhi, Homi Bhabha, Raja Ramanna.
  • Note: Science, non-alignment in foreign policy.
1975–1977 Pan-India Emergency
  • Overview: Indira Gandhi declares Emergency, citing “internal disturbance.”
  • Significance: Suspended civil liberties; media censorship; mass sterilizations.
  • Key Figures: Indira Gandhi, Sanjay Gandhi, J.P. Narayan.
  • Note: Article 356 misuse, constitutional crisis.
1977 Pan-India First Non-Congress Government
  • Overview: Janata Party forms government post-Emergency.
  • Significance: Ended Congress dominance; Morarji Desai becomes PM.
  • Key Figures: Morarji Desai, J.P. Narayan, Charan Singh.
  • Note: Coalition politics origin, political shift.
1980 Pan-India Mandal Commission Report
  • Overview: Recommends 27% reservation for OBCs in government jobs.
  • Significance: Sparked caste debates; implemented in 1990.
  • Key Figures: B.P. Mandal, V.P. Singh (later).
  • Note: Social justice, reservation policy.
1984 Amritsar, Punjab Operation Blue Star
  • Overview: Army storms Golden Temple to remove Sikh militants.
  • Significance: Led to anti-Sikh riots, Indira Gandhi’s assassination.
  • Key Figures: Indira Gandhi, Jarnail Singh Bhindranwale.
  • Note: Communal tensions, security policy.
1984 Delhi Indira Gandhi’s Assassination
  • Overview: Indira Gandhi assassinated by Sikh bodyguards on October 31.
  • Significance: Triggered anti-Sikh riots; Rajiv Gandhi becomes PM.
  • Key Figures: Indira Gandhi, Rajiv Gandhi.
  • Note: Political instability, communal issues.
1991 Pan-India Economic Liberalization
  • Overview: P.V. Narasimha Rao initiates market reforms.
  • Significance: Ended License Raj; boosted FDI, GDP growth to 7%.
  • Key Figures: P.V. Narasimha Rao, Manmohan Singh.
  • Note: Economic reforms cornerstone.
1992 Ayodhya, Uttar Pradesh Babri Masjid Demolition
  • Overview: Kar sevaks demolish Babri Masjid, claiming Ram temple site.
  • Significance: Sparked nationwide communal riots; polarized politics.
  • Key Figures: L.K. Advani, VHP, BJP.
  • Note: Communal politics, secularism debates.
1998 Pokhran, Rajasthan Pokhran-II Nuclear Tests
  • Overview: Operation Shakti confirms nuclear weapons capability.
  • Significance: Strengthened strategic autonomy; led to sanctions, later waived.
  • Key Figures: Atal Bihari Vajpayee, A.P.J. Abdul Kalam.
  • Note: Defense policy, global diplomacy.
1999 Kargil, Jammu and Kashmir Kargil War
  • Overview: India repels Pakistani intrusion in Kargil sector.
  • Significance: Operation Vijay success; highlighted nuclear-era tensions.
  • Key Figures: Vajpayee, Indian Army.
  • Note: Indo-Pak relations, defense strategy.
2005 Pan-India Right to Information Act
  • Overview: RTI Act passed, empowering citizens to access government data.
  • Significance: Enhanced transparency, curbed corruption.
  • Key Figures: Manmohan Singh, Aruna Roy (activist).
  • Note: Governance, anti-corruption measures.
2014 Pan-India Mars Orbiter Mission
  • Overview: ISRO’s Mangalyaan enters Mars orbit, first attempt success.
  • Significance: India joins elite space nations; cost-effective at $74M.
  • Key Figures: ISRO, K. Radhakrishnan.
  • Note: Science and technology, global prestige.
2017 Pan-India Goods and Services Tax (GST)
  • Overview: GST unifies indirect taxes into single tax system.
  • Significance: Simplified tax structure; boosted economic integration.
  • Key Figures: Narendra Modi, Arun Jaitley.
  • Note: Economic reform, federal cooperation.
2019 Jammu and Kashmir Abrogation of Article 370
  • Overview: Article 370 revoked, ending J&K’s special status.
  • Significance: J&K reorganized into two UTs; sparked global debate.
  • Key Figures: Narendra Modi, Amit Shah.
  • Note: Federalism, constitutional changes.

First Kashmir War October 1947–January 1949 Jammu & Kashmir India vs. Pakistan, tribal militias
  • Defend Kashmir after Pakistan-backed tribal invasion post-Partition.
  • Secure Jammu & Kashmir’s accession to India (1947).
  • Ceasefire (January 1, 1949) via UN mediation.
  • Line of Control (LoC) established; Kashmir divided.
  • India retained ~65% of Jammu & Kashmir.
  • Lt. Gen. K.M. Cariappa (Indian Army Chief).
  • Maj. Gen. Kulwant Singh (J&K operations).
Sino-Indian War October–November 1962 Aksai Chin, Arunachal Pradesh India vs. China
  • Resolve border disputes over Aksai Chin and McMahon Line.
  • Counter Chinese incursions after 1959 Tibet uprising.
  • Chinese victory; unilateral ceasefire (November 21, 1962).
  • China retained Aksai Chin, withdrew from Arunachal Pradesh.
  • India’s military overhaul initiated.
  • Lt. Gen. B.M. Kaul (IV Corps).
  • Gen. P.N. Thapar (Army Chief).
Indo-Pak War August–September 1965 Jammu & Kashmir, Rann of Kutch India vs. Pakistan
  • Counter Pakistan’s Operation Gibraltar to infiltrate Kashmir.
  • Defend territorial integrity after Rann of Kutch skirmishes (April 1965).
  • Ceasefire (September 23, 1965) via UN mediation.
  • Tashkent Agreement (January 1966); no territorial changes.
  • Stalemate; India’s military resilience proven.
  • Gen. J.N. Chaudhuri (Army Chief).
  • Lt. Gen. Harbakhsh Singh (Western Command).
Bangladesh Liberation War March–December 1971 East Pakistan (now Bangladesh) India, Mukti Bahini vs. Pakistan
  • Support Bangladesh’s independence from Pakistan’s oppression.
  • Counter refugee crisis (10M fled to India) and Pakistan’s genocide.
  • Respond to Pakistan’s pre-emptive strikes (December 3, 1971).
  • Decisive Indian victory; Pakistan surrendered (December 16, 1971).
  • Bangladesh created as an independent nation.
  • Simla Agreement (1972) redefined LoC.
  • Gen. Sam Manekshaw (Army Chief).
  • Lt. Gen. J.S. Aurora (Eastern Command).
Kargil War May–July 1999 Kargil, Jammu & Kashmir India vs. Pakistan
  • Evict Pakistani regulars and militants infiltrating across LoC.
  • Secure strategic heights in Kargil to protect Srinagar-Leh highway.
  • Indian victory; all intrusions cleared by July 26, 1999.
  • Status quo ante restored; no territorial changes.
  • Strengthened India’s global diplomatic stance.
  • Gen. V.P. Malik (Army Chief).
  • Lt. Gen. Y.K. Joshi (3rd Infantry Division).

Event Time Frame Location Purpose Details
Riots
Communal Riots (Post-Partition) 1947–1948 Delhi, Punjab, Bengal Religious vengeance
  • Triggered by Partition, Hindu-Muslim riots killed thousands and displaced millions.
  • Gandhi’s peace marches and government crackdowns restored order.
  • Deepened communal divide, shaping early India-Pakistan relations.
Nellie Massacre 1983 Nellie, Assam Ethnic–communal violence during Assam Agitation
  • Mass killing of Bengali-speaking Muslims in Morigaon district.
  • Occurred during tensions over voter rolls and alleged illegal migration.
  • Identified as one of the deadliest massacres in independent India.
  • Over 40 villages attacked by mobs.
Anti-Sikh Riots 1984 Delhi, Punjab Retaliation for Indira Gandhi’s assassination
  • Congress-led mobs killed ~3,000 Sikhs after Gandhi’s killing by Sikh bodyguards.
  • Judicial inquiries exposed state complicity; few convictions achieved.
  • Fueled Sikh alienation and strengthened diaspora support for Khalistan.
Babri Masjid Riots 1992–1993 Uttar Pradesh, Mumbai Communal polarization

Political: Parliamentary Democracy 1950–Present Nationwide
  • Structure: Bicameral Parliament (Lok Sabha, Rajya Sabha) with President as head of state.
    • Lok Sabha (lower house): 543 elected MPs; Rajya Sabha (upper house): 245 members (233 elected, 12 nominated).
    • Legislative powers divided via Union, State, and Concurrent Lists (Seventh Schedule).
  • Functioning: Government led by Prime Minister; Council of Ministers accountable to Lok Sabha.
  • Features: Universal adult suffrage; multi-party system; separation of powers (legislature, executive, judiciary).
  • Constitutional Basis: Part V of Constitution; ensures democratic accountability.
  • President: Ceremonial head; supreme commander of armed forces.
  • Prime Minister: Head of government; chairs Council of Ministers.
  • Speaker (Lok Sabha): Presides over lower house; ensures orderly proceedings.
  • Chairman (Rajya Sabha): Vice-President; presides over upper house.
  • Cabinet Ministers: Head key ministries (e.g., Finance, Home).
Political: Federal Structure 1950–Present Nationwide
  • Structure: Union of 28 states and 8 union territories with asymmetric federalism.
    • States have legislatures (unicameral/bicameral); UTs governed by central appointees or elected assemblies.
    • Division of powers via Union, State, and Concurrent Lists.
  • Mechanisms: Inter-State Council, Finance Commission for fiscal federalism.
  • Features: Strong center with emergency powers (Article 356); cooperative federalism via GST Council, NITI Aayog.
  • Constitutional Basis: Part XI; balances unity and regional autonomy.
  • President: Appoints Governors; approves state bills.
  • Governor: State head; links state to center.
  • Chief Minister: Head of state government; leads Council of Ministers.
  • Finance Commission Chairman: Allocates fiscal resources to states.
Political: Electoral System 1950–Present Nationwide
  • Structure: Managed by Election Commission of India (ECI); first-past-the-post system.
    • Lok Sabha and state assembly elections every 5 years; Rajya Sabha staggered (1/3rd retire every 2 years).
    • Voter eligibility: 18+ citizens; universal suffrage.
  • Features: Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs) since 2004; Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail (VVPAT).
  • Mechanisms: Model Code of Conduct; delimitation for constituencies.
  • Constitutional Basis: Part XV; ensures free and fair elections.
  • Chief Election Commissioner: Heads ECI; oversees elections.
  • Election Commissioners: Assist CEC in electoral management.
  • Returning Officer: Manages polling at constituency level.
  • District Election Officer: Coordinates district-level elections.
Administrative: Civil Services 1947–Present Nationwide
  • Structure: All-India Services (IAS, IPS, IFoS) and Central/State Services; evolved from ICS.
    • Recruited via UPSC (central) and SPSC (state) exams; cadre-based allocation.
    • Hierarchical: Secretary (apex), Joint Secretary, Deputy Secretary, etc.
  • Functions: Policy implementation, public administration, law enforcement (IPS).
  • Features: Permanent bureaucracy; merit-based; transferable postings.
  • Constitutional Basis: Part XIV; Articles 308–323 for services and UPSC.
  • Cabinet Secretary: Apex civil servant; coordinates ministries.
  • Chief Secretary: Heads state administration; links to center.
  • District Magistrate (DM): IAS officer; manages district administration.
  • Superintendent of Police (SP): IPS officer; heads district police.
Administrative: Panchayati Raj 1992–Present Rural Areas
  • Structure: Three-tier system (Gram Panchayat, Panchayat Samiti, Zila Parishad) for local self-governance.
    • Gram Sabha as decision-making body; elected representatives at each tier.
    • Devolves 29 subjects (e.g., agriculture, education) via 11th Schedule.
  • Functions: Rural development, welfare scheme delivery, local planning.
  • Features: 33% women’s reservation; State Election Commissions for elections.
  • Constitutional Basis: 73rd Amendment (1992); Part IX.
  • Sarpanch: Heads Gram Panchayat; leads local governance.
  • Block Development Officer (BDO): Oversees Panchayat Samiti.
  • Zila Parishad Chairman: Heads district-level body.
  • District Magistrate: Coordinates with Zila Parishad.

    • Elected councils; ward-level representation; 18 subjects (e.g., water, sanitation) via 12th Schedule.

The speech highlighted the dawn of a new era, where India would awake to "life and freedom." - Nehru emphasized unity, equality, and the need to remove poverty, ignorance, and disease. Importantly, he acknowledged the challenges ahead, including the trauma of Partition, but reaffirmed India’s commitment to democracy and secularism. - Shared Vision for the Nation - The national movement had diverse voices, but two fundamental goals united all: establishing a democratic government and ensuring the upliftment of the poor and disadvantaged. - With independence achieved, the time had come to fulfill these aspirations. - Challenges of Independence - India's birth came with immense hardships, arguably more severe than any other newly independent nation at the time. - Partition led to unprecedented violence and mass displacement, creating a crisis in the newly formed country. - Resilience Amidst Turmoil - Despite the challenges, India's leaders remained steadfast in their commitment to nation-building. - The struggle for independence was over, but the journey to realizing its promises had only begun. - Challenges Before Independent India - Nation-Building and Unity - The foremost challenge was to create a unified nation while respecting India's vast diversity. - India's continental size, multiple languages, cultures, and religions made national integration difficult. - The partition of 1947 raised fears about India's ability to remain united. - Questions arose about whether unity would come at the cost of regional and sub-national identities. - A crucial task was integrating the princely states and ensuring territorial consolidation. - Establishing Democracy - India adopted a parliamentary democracy with universal suffrage (right to vote) and fundamental rights. - A democratic constitution alone was not enough; the challenge was to instill (gradually but firmly establish) democratic practices. - Political competition had to be conducted within a democratic framework, ensuring representation and participation. - Socio-Economic Development - Development had to benefit the entire society, not just a privileged few. - The Constitution guaranteed equality and protection for socially disadvantaged and minority groups. - The Two-Nation Theory and the Demand for Pakistan - The Muslim League advanced the two-nation theory, asserting that India was home to not one but two distinct communities—Hindus and Muslims. - Based on this theory, the League demanded the creation of Pakistan as a separate nation for Muslims. - The partition of British India resulted in the creation of two separate nations—India and Pakistan. - The division followed the principle of religious majorities, meaning areas where Muslims were in the majority would become Pakistan, while the rest would remain part of India. - Though the idea seemed straightforward, its execution proved to be extremely complex and painful. - Geographical Challenges - Muslim-majority areas in British India were not contiguous (sharing a common border, adjacent); instead, they were concentrated in two distinct regions—one in the west and another in the east. - Since these two regions could not be connected, Pakistan was formed as two separate territories—West Pakistan and East Pakistan—separated by a vast expanse of Indian land. - Opposition to Partition - Division of Punjab and Bengal - Punjab and Bengal, though Muslim-majority provinces, had significant non-Muslim populations in certain regions. - To address this, both provinces were divided based on religious majorities at the district level or even lower. - However, this decision was not finalized by 14-15 August, leaving many people uncertain about whether they belonged to India or Pakistan. - The partition of these two provinces caused immense suffering, with large-scale violence and displacement. - The Minority Crisis - The most severe and unpredictable problem of partition was the plight of religious minorities on both sides of the border. - Lakhs of Hindus and Sikhs in the newly created Pakistan, and an equal number of Muslims in Indian Punjab, Bengal, and Delhi, found themselves alienated in their own homeland. - As soon as partition became imminent, minorities became targets of communal violence, escalating beyond control. - No concrete plans were in place to manage the crisis, and people were forced to flee their homes within hours, leaving behind everything they owned. - Impact of Partition - Mass Migration and Violence - 1947 witnessed one of history's largest and most abrupt unplanned population transfers. - Widespread killings and atrocities occurred on both sides in the name of religion. - Cities like Lahore, Amritsar, and Kolkata became divided into 'communal zones'. - Hindus, Sikhs, and Muslims avoided areas dominated by other communities. - - Displacement and Suffering - Minorities were forced to flee their homes and seek shelter in refugee camps. - The local administration and police often failed to provide protection. - People migrated by foot, trains, or any available means, facing attacks along the way. - Thousands of women were abducted, forcibly converted, and married off. - Many families killed their own women to protect 'family honour'. - Children were separated from their parents; many refugees remained homeless for years. - Cultural Reflection of Partition - Writers, poets, and filmmakers in India and Pakistan captured the trauma of Partition. - Survivors often described it as a 'division of hearts'. - Division Beyond Land - Partition was not just about territorial separation; it also divided financial assets and resources. - Government and railway employees were split between the two nations. - Declaration of Emergency - Allahabad High Court Judgment on Indira Gandhi's Election - Background: - On 12 June 1975, Justice Jagmohan Lal Sinha of the Allahabad High Court declared Indira Gandhi's election to the Lok Sabha invalid. - The decision was based on an election petition filed by Raj Narain, a socialist leader who had contested against her in the 1971 elections. - The petition alleged that Indira Gandhi had misused the services of government servants during her campaign. - Impact of the Judgment: - The High Court's ruling meant that Indira Gandhi was no longer an MP and, as a result, could not continue as Prime Minister unless re-elected within six months. - Supreme Court's Partial Stay: - Government's Response: - Faced with mounting pressure, the government declared a state of emergency on 25 June 1975. - The government cited a threat of internal disturbances and invoked Article 352 of the Constitution, which allows for the declaration of an emergency due to either an external threat or internal disturbances. - Emergency Powers: - The declaration of emergency granted the government extraordinary powers, including: - Suspension of the federal distribution of powers, centralizing authority with the union government. - The ability to curtail (reduce in extent or quantity) or restrict Fundamental Rights during the emergency. - The emergency status was seen as a condition where normal democratic processes could not function, and thus, special powers were granted. - Immediate Actions: - On the night of 25 June 1975, Indira Gandhi recommended the imposition of the emergency to President Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed, who issued the proclamation immediately. - The cabinet was not consulted beforehand, which many critics viewed as a bypass of democratic norms. The proclamation was later ratified by the Parliament, where Indira's government had a majority. - After midnight, electricity was cut off to major newspaper offices, and opposition leaders and workers were arrested. - The Cabinet was informed about these developments at a special meeting on 26 June at 6 a.m.. - Impact/Consequences of Emergency and Government Actions - Political Repression and Control: - The declaration of Emergency brought a sudden halt to the opposition agitation, with strikes being banned and many opposition leaders arrested. - The political atmosphere became quiet, but tense. - Press Censorship: - The government, using its special powers under the Emergency provisions, suspended freedom of the press. - Newspapers were required to obtain prior approval for all material to be published, enforcing press censorship. - Suppression of Social and Political Groups: - Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS) and Jamait-e-Islami were banned, citing concerns over social and communal disharmony. - Protests, strikes, and public agitations were also prohibited. - Most significantly, Fundamental Rights were suspended, including citizens' rights to approach the Court for the restoration of their rights. - Preventive Detention: - The government extensively used preventive detention to arrest and detain individuals without charge or evidence of an offence, based on the assumption they might commit one. - Arrested individuals could not challenge their detention via habeas corpus petitions. - Habeas corpus is an equitable remedy by which a report can be made to a court alleging the unlawful detention or imprisonment of an individual, and requesting that the court order the individual's custodian to bring the prisoner to court, to determine whether their detention is lawful. - The Supreme Court, in April 1976, overruled the High Courts' rulings that allowed habeas corpus petitions during an emergency, effectively giving the government the power to deny citizens' right to life and liberty during this period. - This judgment is considered one of the most controversial in Indian judicial history, as it closed the doors of the judiciary to citizens during the Emergency. - Acts of Dissent and Resistance: - Despite the oppressive measures, many political workers went underground to organize protests. - Journalists who spoke out against the Emergency were arrested. - Underground publications were created to bypass censorship. - Notable writers, such as Shivarama Karanth and Fanishwarnath Renu, returned their Padma awards in protest against the suspension of democracy. - Arrested individuals were not tried for anti-national activities, and no cases were registered against most detainees. - The Home Ministry, responsible for monitoring law and order, did not express concern over the situation. The critics argued that the government had sufficient routine powers to address the agitations. - They claimed the real threat was to the ruling party and Indira Gandhi's personal power, not to the country's unity or integrity. - Critics contend that Indira Gandhi misused the constitutional provision meant for saving the country to protect her political position. - Additional side information - Shah Commission of Inquiry: - In May 1977, the Janata Party government appointed a Commission of Inquiry headed by Justice J.C. However, the cost of the project was massive: - 245 villages across Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat, and Maharashtra were expected to submerge. - Around 2.5 lakh people were forced to relocate. - Local activist groups first raised concerns about inadequate rehabilitation of displaced communities, leading to the formal launch of NBA in 1988-89. -
