- Pillar 2D3: Budget → Types of Budget and Schemes - Introduction: - Budgets in India are classified based on their purpose, funding, and expenditure types, guiding resource allocation and fiscal policy. Understanding these classifications and their management is critical for assessing government efficiency and economic impact. - Revenue vs. Capital Budget - Revenue Budget: - Covers short-term (1 year or less), non-asset-creating income and expenditure. - Includes: - Taxes, non-tax revenue (e.g., fees, dividends), and revenue expenditure (e.g., salaries, pensions, subsidies, interest payments). - Capital Budget: - Covers long-term, asset-creating income and expenditure. - Includes: - Borrowings, disinvestment proceeds, and capital expenditure (e.g., infrastructure, machinery, financial assets like bonds). -
Budget Type Nature Examples
Revenue Budget Short-term, non-asset-creating Taxes, salaries, subsidies
Capital Budget Long-term, asset-creating Borrowings, infrastructure, disinvestment
- General Budget vs. Railway Budget - Historical Context: - 1920–21: - Acworth Committee recommended a separate Railway Budget due to railways’ significant revenue contribution during British rule. - 1924: - Separation Convention formalized this practice. - Post-independence, the Railway Budget was presented before the General Budget. - Merger (2017): - Modi government merged the Railway Budget with the General Budget, based on NITI Aayog’s Bibek Debroy Committee recommendations: - No constitutional requirement for a separate budget. - Populist measures (e.g., low fares) during coalition governments eroded railway profitability. - Railway revenue became a small fraction of the General Budget, not justifying separate presentation. - Impact: - Streamlined budgeting, reduced populism, and aligned railway finances with national fiscal goals. - Plan vs. Non-Plan Expenditure Budget - Definition: - Plan Expenditure: - Linked to Five-Year Plans (e.g., development projects). - Non-Plan Expenditure: - Routine expenses (e.g., salaries, maintenance). - Discontinuation (Budget 2017): - Modi government ended the distinction due to: - No constitutional mandate. - Dissolution of the Planning Commission (2014–15). - End of the 12th Five-Year Plan (2012–17). - Impact: - Simplified budget presentation, focusing on revenue and capital classifications. - Budgeting Methods - Traditional/Line-Item Budgeting: - Focuses on listing income and expenditure without evaluating benefits (e.g., Rs. 10,000 for hospital beds). - Lacks performance measurement. - Performance Budgeting: - Ties expenditure to measurable outcomes (e.g., Rs. 50,000 for computers to achieve 30% faster RTI processing). - Enhances cost-benefit analysis and efficiency. - Zero-Based Budgeting (ZBB): - Requires departments to justify all expenditure from scratch, not incrementally (e.g., reviewing education schemes’ effectiveness before reallocation). - Prevents automatic budget increases. - Sunset Budgeting: - Schemes have predefined deadlines (e.g., a subsidy for two years), ensuring automatic termination unless renewed. -

- Gender Budget - Definition: - A statement within the General Budget (since 2005) highlighting allocations for women-centric schemes, though not constitutionally mandated. - Structure (Budget 2025): - Part A: - 100% women-focused schemes (e.g., SAMBAL, SAMARTHYA under Women and Child Development Ministry) – >Rs. 1 lakh crore. - Part B: - Schemes with at least 30% allocation for women (e.g., school education) – >Rs. 3 lakh crore. - Part C (since Budget 2024–July): - Schemes with less than 30% for women (e.g., PM-KISAN, Rs. 6,000 to farmers) – >Rs. 16,000 crore. - Total: - >Rs. 4 lakh crore. - Criticism: - Three-part system (introduced in 2024) accused of data manipulation to exaggerate women-focused spending. -

- Tribal Sub-Plan (TSP) and Scheduled Castes Sub-Plan (SCSP) - Definition: - Since the 1970s, ministries must earmark funds for Scheduled Castes (SC) and Scheduled Tribes (ST) within their budgets. - Monitoring: - SCSP: - Social Justice Ministry via e-utthaan.gov.in. - TSP: - Tribal Affairs Ministry via stcmis.gov.in. - Additional Allocations: - Budget documents also include allocations for children and North Eastern Areas (not constitutionally required). - Output-Outcome Framework for Schemes - Introduced: - Budget 2017. - Purpose: - Tracks scheme outlays, outputs (deliverables), and outcomes (impact), monitored by NITI Aayog. - Example: -

- Cash vs. Accrual Budgeting - Note: - Outdated topic, removed from recent handouts. Cash budgeting records transactions when cash changes hands; accrual budgeting records when obligations are incurred. Not relevant for Prelims. - Lapsable Funds and March Rush - Definition: - Appropriation Act allows spending from the Consolidated Fund of India within one financial year (ending March 31). Unspent funds lapse and require fresh parliamentary approval. - March Rush: - Departments rush to spend funds in March to avoid lapse, often leading to inefficient or reckless expenditure. - Mitigation (2017–18): - Finance Ministry capped fourth-quarter spending at 33% and March spending at 15% of annual budget to curb wasteful spending. - Non-Lapsable Funds - Definition: - Funds that do not lapse on March 31, usable without fresh parliamentary approval (e.g., Nirbhaya Fund for women’s safety). - Criticism: - Lax utilization; e.g., Nirbhaya Fund (2013) had ~Rs. 3,000 crore by 2018, with less than 50% utilized. - 2018: - Defence Ministry’s request for a non-lapsable modernization fund rejected due to utilization concerns. - 15th Finance Commission: - Recommended Modernisation Fund for Defence and Internal Security (MFDIS) of Rs. 2.38 lakh crore (2021–26). -

- Types of Schemes - Classification: - Centrally Sponsored Schemes (CSS): - Shared funding between Union and States. - Central Sector Schemes: - Fully funded by the Union. - Union Territory Schemes (without legislature): - 100% Union-funded. - Management: - Disbursed and monitored via Public Financial Management System (PFMS) by Controller General of Accounts (CGA), Department of Expenditure. - Budget 2022 CSS Rationalization: - Reduced CSS from 130 to 65 to eliminate duplication and improve efficiency. - Examples: - Stopped: - National Dairy Plan-II, Dairying Through Cooperatives. - Merged: - Women and Child Development Ministry: - 19 schemes merged into Mission Shakti, Mission Vatsalya, Saksham Anganwadi, and POSHAN 2.0. - Agriculture Ministry: - 20 CSS merged into Krishionati Yojana, Integrated Scheme on Agricultural Cooperative, Rashtriya Krishi Vikas Yojana. - Impact: - Reduced subsidy burden and fiscal deficit through better monitoring. - Ministry-Wise Highest Allocation (Budget 2025) - Note: - Specific data not provided in the prompt; typically, ministries like Defence, Agriculture, and Education receive the highest allocations (in Rs. crore). Refer to Budget 2025 documents for exact figures. - Result-Based Financing - Definition: - Shifts from input-based financing (funding inputs like materials) to result-based financing (funding tied to outcomes, e.g., 15 km road constructed daily or 80% skill trainees employed). - Budget 2023: - Adopted for select schemes to ensure accountability, with penalties for unmet targets. -
