- Pillar 2B3: Revenue Expenditure - Definition: - Revenue expenditure refers to government spending on the day-to-day operations and maintenance of state functions, which does not create long-term income-generating or permanent assets. It includes expenses like salaries, pensions, subsidies, and interest payments on loans, critical for sustaining administrative, social, and economic services. - Context: - Revenue expenditure forms a significant portion of India’s Union Budget, ensuring the smooth functioning of executive, judiciary, legislature, and statutory bodies while supporting welfare schemes through subsidies and grants. Efficient management is vital to balance fiscal health and public welfare. - Significance for India: - Revenue expenditure supports essential services like defense, education, and healthcare, and funds welfare programs like subsidies for food, fuel, and fertilizers. It is crucial for maintaining governance and social equity but must be controlled to prevent fiscal deficits and ensure sustainable economic growth. - Overview - Definition: - Revenue expenditure encompasses routine expenses that maintain government operations without creating capital assets, such as salaries, pensions, utilities (e.g., electricity, stationery), and interest payments on loans. - Characteristics: - Does not result in income-generating or permanent assets (unlike capital expenditure, which creates assets like roads or buildings). - Funds the operational costs of executive, judiciary, legislature, constitutional bodies (e.g., Election Commission), and statutory bodies (e.g., SEBI). - Includes payments for social services (health, education), economic services (agriculture, transport), and administrative functions (police, external affairs). - Key Components: - Interest Payments: - Payments on interest for loans (principal repayment is capital expenditure). This is the largest revenue expenditure, reflecting India’s debt servicing obligations. - Subsidies: - Financial assistance to reduce the burden on citizens or industries (e.g., food, fertilizer, fuel subsidies). - Salaries and Pensions: - Compensation for government employees and retirees, including dearness allowances to offset inflation. - Grants-in-Aid: - Funds provided to states, local bodies, or institutions (e.g., disaster management, Panchayati Raj) without repayment obligations. - Defense Expenditure: - Salaries, fuel, and maintenance for armed forces (e.g., fuel for tanks, soldier salaries). - Union Territories without Legislature: - Operational expenses for UTs like Chandigarh and Lakshadweep. -

- Subsidies - Definition: - Subsidies are financial benefits provided by the government to individuals, farmers, or industries to reduce costs, promote welfare, or support economic activities. Unlike taxes, subsidies are voluntary and not punishable if refused. - Comparison with Taxes: -
Aspect Tax Subsidy
Nature Compulsory contribution to the state; non-payment is punishable. Voluntary benefit to reduce burden; refusal is not penalized.
Benefit No direct or specific goods/services guaranteed (e.g., Mukesh Ambani’s taxes don’t ensure pothole-free roads). Provides specific benefits (e.g., Rs. 6,000 under PM-KISAN, subsidized LPG).
- Types of Subsidies - Definition: - Subsidies are categorized based on their delivery mechanism and purpose, aimed at promoting affordability, equity, or economic growth. - Types and Examples: - Direct Cash Subsidies: - Transferred directly to beneficiaries’ bank accounts (e.g., PM-KISAN: Rs. 6,000 annually for farmers; DBT-PAHAL: ~Rs. 200 per LPG cylinder). - In-Kind Subsidies: - Non-monetary benefits like free school bags, uniforms, books for poor students, free medicines in public hospitals, or free insurance schemes (e.g., Ayushman Bharat). - Indirect Subsidies: - Government pays organizations to offer goods/services at reduced rates (e.g., low fees in government colleges, subsidized kerosene, urea, or crop insurance premiums). - Implicit Subsidies: - Government policies suppress supply to raise prices for a sector’s benefit (e.g., banning American poultry imports creates a shortage, allowing Indian poultry farmers to charge higher prices, indirectly subsidizing them). - Cross-Subsidization: - One group’s higher payments offset another’s lower costs (e.g., Indian Railways charges higher freight rates to keep passenger fares affordable for the poor). - Regulatory Subsidies: - Mandated price caps by regulators (e.g., State Electricity Regulatory Commission limits electricity rates for farmers to Rs. 2–4 per unit). - Procurement Subsidies: - Government purchases at above-market prices (e.g., Food Corporation of India buys food grains at Minimum Support Price to support farmers). - Interest Subsidies (Subvention): - Government pays part of the interest on loans for farmers, MSMEs, or affordable housing (e.g., LIC’s Vaya Vandana Yojana for senior citizens). -

- Impact of Subsidies - Positive Impacts: - Merit Goods: - Subsidies on healthcare, education, LPG, solar panels, and windmills increase access and generate positive externalities (e.g., LPG subsidies reduce firewood use, preserving trees and reducing indoor air pollution). - Economic Support: - Subsidies like PM-KISAN and fertilizer subsidies stabilize farmers’ incomes, enhancing agricultural productivity and rural welfare. - Social Equity: - In-kind subsidies (e.g., free school supplies) and indirect subsidies (e.g., cheap college fees) promote access to education and healthcare for the poor. - Negative Impacts: - Environmental Harm: - Subsidies on diesel, kerosene, and urea lead to negative externalities like air pollution, soil degradation, water pollution, and algae blooms due to overuse. - Subsidy Leakage: - Inefficiencies like ghost beneficiaries (non-existent recipients created by corrupt officials) and ineligible beneficiaries (e.g., wealthy individuals receiving subsidies) waste resources. - Fiscal Burden: - High subsidy costs strain government budgets, potentially increasing fiscal deficits (e.g., food subsidies alone cost ~Rs. 2 lakh crore in Budget 2024–25). - Market Distortion: - Implicit subsidies (e.g., poultry import bans) and procurement subsidies (e.g., MSP) can distort market prices, affecting economic efficiency. -

- Subsidy Delivery → JAM and DBT - Definition: - JAM (Jan Dhan, Aadhaar, Mobile) and Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) are mechanisms to deliver subsidies efficiently by linking bank accounts, Aadhaar, and mobile numbers to reduce leakage and ensure benefits reach intended recipients. - Economic Survey Observations (2015–23): - 2015–16: - JAM introduced to streamline subsidy delivery, reducing middlemen and ghost beneficiaries. - 2016–17: - DBT saved ~Rs. 36,000 crore by eliminating fake LPG connections. - 2018–19: - Highlighted DBT’s role in food subsidies (e.g., PDS) and MGNREGA payments, ensuring transparency. - 2022–23: - DBT expanded to 300+ schemes, transferring ~Rs. 6.3 lakh crore annually, with Aadhaar-based authentication minimizing fraud. - Process: - Beneficiaries’ bank accounts are linked to Aadhaar and mobile numbers. - Subsidies (e.g., PM-KISAN, LPG) are transferred directly to accounts, bypassing intermediaries. - Example: - PAHAL scheme transfers LPG subsidies to consumers’ accounts, reducing black-market sales of cylinders. - Challenges: - Digital divide in rural areas limits access to mobile/internet for DBT. - Aadhaar authentication failures due to biometric mismatches exclude genuine beneficiaries. - High administrative costs for maintaining DBT infrastructure. - National Recruitment Agency (NRA) - Definition: - Established in 2020 under Budget 2020, NRA conducts a Common Eligibility Test (CET) to shortlist candidates for non-gazetted Group B and C posts in central government and public sector banks (PSBs). - Objectives: - Streamline recruitment by replacing multiple agency exams with a single CET, reducing time and cost for candidates and recruiters. - SSC and IBPS conduct subsequent mains exams for specific posts. - Features: - Computerized test centers in every district for accessibility. - CET scores valid for multiple agencies, simplifying the application process. - Focus on aspirational districts (117) to enhance employment opportunities. - Significance: - Reduces duplication of exams (e.g., SSC, IBPS, Railways) and lowers financial burden on candidates. - Enhances transparency and efficiency in government recruitment. - Note: - NRA is not a constitutional body and was not established in 2014, nor does it facilitate private-sector jobs. - Revenue Expenditure → Salaries - Definition: - Salaries and related allowances for central government employees and retirees form a significant portion of revenue expenditure, ensuring workforce compensation and inflation protection. - 7th Pay Commission (2014–16): - Overview: - Constituted by the Ministry of Finance (Department of Expenditure) under Justice A.K. Mathur, effective from 1 January 2016. The first commission was led by Srinivasa Varadachariar (1946). - Key Recommendations: - Introduced Pay Matrix, replacing pay bands and grade pay for simplified salary structures. - Increased minimum salary to Rs. 18,000/month (Group D) and maximum to Rs. 2.25 lakh/month (Apex scale, e.g., Department Secretary) and Rs. 2.5 lakh/month (Cabinet Secretary). - Regulatory body salaries raised (e.g., Chairman: Rs. 4.5 lakh/month). - Adopted Dr. Aykroyd’s formula to adjust wages based on commodity price changes, potentially eliminating the need for future pay commissions. - Strengthened Modified Assured Career Progression (MACP) to ensure merit-based promotions, curbing automatic promotions for underperforming officials. - Reforms for defense and Central Armed Police Forces (CAPF), including better allowances and service conditions. -

- 8th Pay Commission (Budget 2025) - Overview: - Announced in Budget 2025, but chairman details were unavailable as of 1 February 2025. Aims to further refine salary structures and address inflation and employee welfare. - Context: - Likely to build on the 7th Pay Commission’s Pay Matrix and Aykroyd formula, ensuring dynamic salary adjustments. - Expected to address concerns like rising living costs and pension disparities. - Dearness Allowance (DA) and Dearness Relief (DR) - Definition: - Dearness Allowance (DA) is a cost-of-living adjustment for serving central government employees to offset inflation. Dearness Relief (DR) is a similar allowance for retired employees. - Mechanism: - Calculated as a percentage of basic salary/pension, based on the All India Consumer Price Index (AICPI). - Revised biannually (January and July) by the Department of Expenditure. - Key Developments: - 2020: - DA/DR frozen due to COVID-19 fiscal constraints. - 2021 (July): - Resumed at 28% (from 17%), with arrears paid to employees and pensioners. - 2024: - DA increased to 50% of basic salary for ~1 crore employees and pensioners, reflecting inflation adjustments. -

- Bonus to Government Employees - Definition: - Bonuses are additional payments beyond regular salaries, given occasionally to boost morale or reward performance. - Types: - Productivity-Linked Bonus (PLB): - Tied to measurable output (e.g., files cleared, trees planted, or sales improved in postal/railways departments). - Non-Productivity-Linked (Ad-Hoc) Bonus: - Given regardless of performance, e.g., Diwali bonus (up to Rs. 7,000 for non-gazetted employees in 2024). - Example: - 2024: - Productivity-linked bonuses for railway employees based on train operation efficiency; ad-hoc Diwali bonuses for Group C and D staff. - Miscellaneous Terms - House Rent Allowance (HRA): - Provided to cover accommodation costs, varying by city classification (e.g., 27% of basic salary in metro cities like Delhi, 9% in smaller towns under 7th Pay Commission). - Gratuity: - Lump-sum payment for employees with continuous service (typically 5+ years), governed by the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972. Maximum limit: Rs. 20 lakh (2024). - One Rank One Pension (OROP): - Introduced in 2015 for military personnel to ensure equal pensions for the same rank and service length, regardless of retirement date. Criticisms include disputes over base year and calculation formulas, with ex-servicemen seeking revisions. -
