- Pillar 2B2: Black​ money & Allied Issues - Definitions and concepts: - Black​ money: - black​ money refers to income or transactions that are taxable but deliberately concealed from tax authorities to evade taxes. It operates within a parallel economy, undermining fiscal integrity and reducing government revenue for welfare and development. Unlike legal tax planning (e.g., investing in LIC or PPF for deductions), which is ethical and lawful, black​ money involves illegal practices like tax​ evasion or money​ laundering. - Tax Planning / Tax Mitigation: - Legally reducing tax liability using provisions like LIC, PPF, pension funds, etc.; it is legal and ethical. - tax​ evasion: - Illegally hiding income or transactions to avoid tax (e.g., not declaring rental income); punishable under law. - Tax Avoidance: - Legally disclosing income but exploiting loopholes to reduce tax (e.g., profit shifting via shell companies); legal but often unethical. - Tax Haven: - Countries with low/zero tax and secrecy laws (e.g., Panama, Mauritius, Cayman Islands) that attract foreign individuals or firms seeking to avoid/evade taxes. - Money​ laundering: - Definition: - The process of converting “dirty money” (illegally earned) into “clean money” (legally usable). - Involves disguising the origin, ownership, or destination of money obtained through criminal activities. - money​ laundering generally presumes criminal origin, especially under Indian and international laws. - Stages: - Placement: - Introducing illegal money into the financial system (e.g., via casinos, deposits). - Layering: - Complex transactions to obscure the money trail (e.g., shell companies). - Integration: - Reintroducing laundered money into the economy as clean (e.g., real estate, luxury purchases). - Tools: - Real estate, shell companies, hawala, foreign accounts, gold. - Hawala: - An informal and illegal system of money transfer that operates outside conventional banking or financial channels. - Money is transferred without physical movement; instead, it relies on a network of brokers or agents (known as hawaladars). - Context: - black​ money distorts economic equity, fuels corruption, and hampers India’s ability to fund public services. India has implemented stringent laws, international agreements, and digital reforms to combat black​ money, aligning with global efforts to enhance financial transparency. - Significance for India: - Tackling black​ money is crucial for increasing tax compliance, boosting revenue for infrastructure and social programs, and strengthening India’s global financial standing. It supports economic stability and aligns with initiatives like Atmanirbhar Bharat by fostering a transparent, inclusive economy. - Notable Organisations - Definition: - Several specialized agencies in India and globally work to detect, prevent, and penalize activities related to black​ money, tax​ evasion, and money​ laundering, ensuring compliance with financial regulations. - Key Organisations: -

- Laffer Curve: - Concept: - By Arthur Laffer; high tax rates reduce revenue by discouraging work or encouraging evasion; tax cuts can increase revenue. - Applications: - Modi Budgets (2017+): - Lowest income tax slab cut from 10% to 5%; corporation tax for small companies reduced from 30% to 25% (phased). - Budget 2020: - Optional income tax slabs introduced. - USA Budget 2017: - Corporation tax cut from 35% to 15%. - Tax Buoyancy & Elasticity: - Tax Elasticity: - Measures absolute increase in tax revenue from a tax rate change (e.g., 5% to 15% income tax slab). - Tax Buoyancy: - Measures tax collection growth relative to GDP growth. - Example (ES23): - GST buoyancy = 1.12 (high; grows faster than GDP); pre-GST indirect taxes = 0.99 (low). - Net Tax Revenue of Union: -
