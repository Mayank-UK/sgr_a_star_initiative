- Report and data - UPSC, being an Indian constitutional body, expects answers to be grounded in Indian perspectives, data, and institutions. So, wherever possible, you should quote Indian sources first. These include: - NITI Aayog, Economic Survey, Union Budget, Census, NFHS, NSSO, Ministry reports - RBI, SEBI, NPCI, National Crime Records Bureau (NCRB) - Parliamentary Committee Reports, Law Commission, Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) - PIB - Supeme court important judgements and highlights or CJI statements - Commitee reports - Official dialogues of Bureocrats - Education data – drop out rates, enrollment rates, primary, secondary vs. higher education, reading skills, writing skills data - Health – NFHS data, MMR, IMR, Fertility rates etc. - Economic – GDP, Inflation, Budget related, export–import related and so on - Agriculture – from farm to table → all data related to seeds, irrigation, soil, infrastructure, mechanization, marketing, middle men, horticulture, rabi, kharif, fishery etc. - Animal husbandry, Irrigation, Infrastructure, Service Sector, Demographic related data - Additional data on various aspects like examples of UPSC frauds, etc - Quotes/Statements from various important people like chief justice of India, attorney journal, defense of staff, RBI governor, etc - Collect more data points based on mains pyq analysis - Collect data for latest release as well as previous 2 release - Using these shows that your answer is contextually relevant and policy-aware, which UPSC values highly. - Collect the remarks of important people of India on important topics like health, defence, education, etc, at present like Ajeet Doval, Prime Minister Narendra Modi, Chief of Defence, Chief Justice, etc - Organize data in categories like education, women, economy, family, sc&st, infrastructure, old people, health, business, employment, etc and memorise - What Top Toppers Say - AIR 1 Tina Dabi – Always begin with Indian data; use global indices to validate or contrast. - AIR 5 Jagrati Awasthi – “Even if quoting the World Bank, connect it to India’s internal policy efforts.” - Mentors like Mrunal, Sriram IAS, ForumIAS – “Indian sources give legitimacy;
